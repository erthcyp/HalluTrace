HalluTrace · project scope · senior project

Where do hallucinations actually come from?

We surveyed twenty-nine hallucination benchmarks, narrowed them to the material we can use, and found a gap none of them fills. This page walks from that survey to the problem it exposed, the solution we propose, what is ours versus borrowed, and how sixteen weeks are spent.

These six leave the pool of material we draw prompts from. They stay in the survey. All six were verified, and all six answer no on the corpus question, so they remain evidence for the gap. Dropping them from the count would shrink the claim from twenty-nine to twenty-three for no reason at all.

03What the survey revealed

01

Twenty-eight of twenty-nine never open the corpus

The near-misses are the instructive part. HalluLens defines extrinsic hallucination as inconsistency with training data and then declines to look — "given the vast and often opaque nature of training data, we chose Wikipedia data in our benchmark." That is a paper from the organization that trained Llama, treating its own corpus as unopenable. HaluEval states the limitation outright: it "does not investigate the underlying reasons behind the appearance of hallucinations."

02

These are not twenty-nine independent attempts — they are roughly five lineages

HaluEval seeds both HaluEval 2.0 and the HaluBench dataset behind Lynx. RAGTruth feeds both Lynx and FaithJudge. FaithBench feeds FaithJudge. FACTS Grounding expands into the FACTS Leaderboard. The Vectara leaderboard evolves in place. If none of the roots inspects pretraining data, every descendant inherits the blind spot by construction — what we document is a small number of foundational decisions propagated forward, not twenty-nine separate oversights.

03

The one paper that asked why has no descendants

HALoGEN is the only benchmark whose categories are defined by their relationship to pretraining data — Type A for misrecall despite the fact being present, Type B for an incorrect fact genuinely in the corpus, Type C for fabrication with no basis. In the derivation graph it sits as an isolated node: it descends from none of the surveyed benchmarks and none descends from it. Over a year on, nobody has built on it.

04The problem

A hallucination can be attacked at exactly three points, and those three differ entirely in cost, in tractability, and in who owns them.

Where you interveneWhat you doOwned by
The corpusRemove or correct the offending documentsWhoever builds the dataset
TrainingIncrease exposure, upweight rare facts, change the objectiveWhoever trains the model
InferenceRetrieval, abstention, decoding strategyWhoever deploys

The remedies are incompatible. If an error came from a bad document, cleaning the corpus fixes it and retrieval cannot — the datastore carries the same mistake. If the fact was present and recall failed, retrieval is exactly the right lever. If nothing was there at all, the only remedy is teaching the model to decline.

The goal

Produce one number that does not currently exist: what fraction of a model's hallucinations could be eliminated by fixing its training data, versus needing intervention during training, or at inference.

Three different teams with three different budgets are all allocating effort against the same population of errors, and none of them knows the split. Everything else in this project is machinery for producing that number.

05Our solution

Label every hallucination twice — once for the kind of failure it is, once for where it came from in the pretraining data — then cross the two labels. Critically, the two labels are decided from different evidence, which is what makes crossing them meaningful rather than circular.

Axis 1 · from the output alone

What kind of failure

  • EXISTENCE — cites something that does not exist
  • ATTRIBUTION — the source is real but does not support the claim
  • NON-ABSTENTION — answers a question that has no correct answer

Checked against PyPI, Semantic Scholar, and a gazetteer. No human judgement needed.

Axis 2 · from the corpus

Where it came from

  • TYPE A — the fact was there; recall failed → training or inference
  • TYPE B — the error is genuinely in the corpus → the corpus
  • TYPE C — nothing was there → abstention only

Two queries per claim into infini-gram; which one returns hits decides the label.

The second group is not left unfinished. It answers a question the first group cannot: is this pattern a quirk of OLMo, or a property of language models? If Llama, Qwen and Mistral fail in similar proportions, our conclusions about cause carry weight beyond the models we could open. Without them, the question "does this only apply to OLMo?" has one honest answer — we don't know.

Their cause columns stay empty, marked n/a — corpus not public rather than left blank. That distinction matters: blank reads as unfinished, n/a reads as impossible. And the empty cells make the project's point better than invented numbers would.

06What we take, what we make

Taken from prior work — cited openly

What the field already had

  • The Type A/B/C concept — HALoGEN
  • Prompt sets — HALoGEN, permissively licensed
  • Reference vs content grounding — HalluHard
  • Non-abstention as a failure mode — HalluLens, AbstentionBench
  • infini-gram as the corpus search tool
  • Models and corpora from AI2 and EleutherAI

Every item here was built to be reused. Using it follows the field's norms rather than cutting a corner — and reusing the prompts is what makes our numbers comparable to HALoGEN's.

Ours

What nobody has done

  • Crossing the two axes — absent from all twenty-nine
  • One decision rule across every task, so results compare
  • The query protocol — how many forms, how combined, how bounds are reported
  • Automatic labeling benchmark-wide, not spot-checks
  • Retaining raw counts → the frequency curve and the Kalai singleton test
  • An error bar on proxy-corpus attribution — nobody has measured this
  • The twenty-nine-paper survey, verified against primary sources
  • The intervention-locus framing — A/B/C as an address, not an explanation
If someone says this is just combining other people's work

This project does not propose a method. It takes a measurement.

HALoGEN established that three causes exist. We establish how much of each there is — moving the claim from existence to magnitude, which is a different kind of scientific statement, not a recombination. Biology does not call an epidemiological survey "just a shovel plus a lab."

And the test that settles it: an assembly cannot produce a wrong answer — a measurement can. Ours can be wrong in several ways. The Kalai prediction may fail. Type B may come back at two percent. OLMo and Pythia may disagree. That the result can be wrong is what proves it is a finding rather than a construction.

W1–2

Feasibility spike

Call infini-gram for real, get a Semantic Scholar key, push fifty prompts through the entire loop until one complete result exists end to end. Gate: if corpus search fails, the plan changes here, not in month three.

W3–4

Assemble tasks

Subsample prompts, author the ground-truth pairs, build the three verifiers. Fix the schema — including the decision to store raw corpus counts rather than thresholded labels.

W5–6

Validate the verifiers

A hundred items per task, two annotators each, agreement and κ reported. Gate: 85% or the task definition narrows.

W7–9

Generation sweep

Seven models, three tasks, three seeds, failure mode labeled automatically. The thesis is viable from this point even if attribution stalls.

W10–13

Attribution

The longest phase, matching where the contribution lies. Query Dolma and the Pile, apply the pre-registered decision tree, hand-inspect a hundred cases.

W14

Analysis

Cross-tabulation, scale curves, frequency bucketing, and the proxy-corpus error bar.

W15–16

Write, and buffer

Thesis, slides, dataset release, and one re-run of the adversarial literature check before submission.

08Questions this keeps getting

Isn't this tool-driven? You had corpus search and invented a question to fit it.

Ask what fraction of hallucination is fixable in the corpus, and open-data models stop being a convenience — they become the only instrument that can answer. The question forces the tool, not the other way around.

Can you run this on GPT, Claude or Gemini?

We can measure them — axis 1 needs only the output. We cannot explain them, because there is no corpus to search. Not by us, not by anyone. This is a closed door rather than a scoping decision.

Then does the finding say anything about the models people actually use?

OLMo and Pythia are model organisms — biology studies mice because it cannot open a living human brain. The analogy holds here better than usual, because these corpora are all built from the same raw web: Common Crawl, Wikipedia, GitHub, Stack Overflow. The flawed posts that mislead OLMo are almost certainly in everyone's training data. We claim a property of web-pretrained models as a class, supported by two independent corpora — not a claim about GPT specifically.

Does this reduce hallucination?

No. It measures and explains; impact arrives when someone else acts on the numbers. "We produce the number this decision requires, and nobody currently has it" is both stronger and defensible.

What if the corpus-fixable share turns out to be tiny?

That is an equally valuable answer, and worth a great deal to anyone currently funding data cleaning. The interpretation rules are fixed in advance precisely so this outcome cannot be read as failure.
