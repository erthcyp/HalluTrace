Built

What nobody has done

  • Crossing the two axes — absent from all 29 surveyed papers
  • One decision rule applied identically across every task, so results compare
  • The query protocol — how many forms, how to combine them, how to report bounds
  • Automatic labeling across a whole benchmark rather than spot-checks
  • Retaining raw counts → the frequency curve and the Kalai test
  • Measuring how wrong proxy-corpus attribution is
  • A 29-paper survey verified against primary sources, one at a time
  • The intervention-locus framing — turning A/B/C from an explanation into an address

03Lines that hold up

If asked · why not invent your own taxonomy

We deliberately did not invent a new taxonomy. The field already has seven mutually incompatible ones; an eighth would make the situation worse, not better. We took the one that answers the question nobody else asks, and made it measurable.

If asked · how is this different from HALoGEN

HALoGEN proposed the concept; we build the instrument. It established that the three causes exist by spot-checking dozens of cases across a few scenarios, using a different method in each, and mostly against proxy corpora. We turn that into one decision rule that labels every instance, against real corpora only, and then cross it with the kind of failure — which HALoGEN never does.

If asked · isn't reusing their prompts a shortcut

Reusing the prompts makes our numbers directly comparable to HALoGEN's, which is a methodological virtue rather than a saving. They published the licences precisely so the material would be reused. Our effort belongs where nothing exists yet, not in rewriting questions that already work.

If pressed · this is just combining other people's work

Yes — and that is precisely why it has not been done. The pieces have been available since HALoGEN appeared over a year ago, but assembling them requires an open-data model, one decision rule that holds across every task, and the discipline to store raw counts from day one. A year on, nobody has assembled them — and HALoGEN itself has no descendants.

04Two positions to fall back on

If the discussion gets difficult enough that every other argument is contested, retreat to these two. Neither requires explaining how it differs from prior work, because neither exists anywhere in the literature to begin with.
