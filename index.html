HalluTrace

Hallucination can be attacked at three points — the pretraining corpus, the training process, or inference — and those three differ entirely in cost, tractability, and who owns them. Nobody knows how a real population of hallucinations distributes across them. This project measures that distribution, which requires opening the corpus and looking.

Problem statement

1 · The failure users cannot see

Language models are now routine tools for finding and synthesizing information, and they continue to produce fluent, confident claims that no evidence supports. The harm lies less in the errors themselves than in their invisibility to the reader. A fabricated citation carries the same typographic authority as a real one. A hallucinated package import runs until it doesn't — and has already been demonstrated as a software supply-chain attack surface. In both cases the surface form gives the user no signal that anything is wrong.

2 · Measurement is no longer the bottleneck

The community has answered with a dense wave of benchmarks. We surveyed twenty-nine, verifying each against its arXiv or ACL Anthology source, and recorded one field above all others: does the paper inspect the pretraining corpus to explain where a hallucination came from?

Surveyed ≠ reused — six benchmarks marked "not reused"

Six of the twenty-nine are outside the reuse pool because their material is not English natural language: HalluQA and C-FAITH (Chinese), HalluScore (Arabic), BHRAM-IL (Indic), HalluBench (cross-lingual), and HalluWorld (synthetic gridworld, chess and terminal states rather than language).

They remain inside the survey. All six were verified and all six answer no on the corpus-inspection field, so they count as evidence for the gap even though we take no prompts from them. Dropping them from the survey would shrink the claim from twenty-nine to twenty-three for no reason.

Twenty-eight of twenty-nine answer no. The near-misses are instructive rather than contradictory. HalluLens defines extrinsic hallucination as inconsistency with training data and then declines to look, stating that "given the vast and often opaque nature of training data, we chose Wikipedia data in our benchmark" — a paper from the organization that trained Llama, treating its own corpus as unopenable. HaluEval 2.0 analyzes hallucination sources across training stages, but by comparing checkpoints and comparing models with differently-documented corpora, and by counting entity frequency in Wikipedia as a proxy. HaluEval states the limitation outright: it "does not investigate the underlying reasons behind the appearance of hallucinations."

3 · Twenty-nine benchmarks, roughly five lineages

These are not twenty-nine independent attempts. Mapping their stated derivations yields roughly five families: HaluEval seeds both HaluEval 2.0 and the HaluBench dataset behind Lynx; RAGTruth feeds both Lynx and FaithJudge; FaithBench feeds FaithJudge; FACTS Grounding expands into the FACTS Leaderboard; and the Vectara HHEM leaderboard evolves in place rather than being superseded. The remainder are largely independent but structurally alike — a prompt set, a verifier, a score.

That concentration matters. If none of the root benchmarks inspects pretraining data, every descendant inherits the blind spot by construction. What this survey documents is therefore not twenty-nine independent oversights but a small number of foundational design decisions propagated forward, unexamined.

The exception proves the point. HALoGEN — the only benchmark whose categories are defined by their relationship to pretraining data — sits in the derivation graph as an isolated node: it descends from none of the surveyed benchmarks, and none descends from it. The one paper that asked why models hallucinate, rather than how often, was never built upon.

4 · Two literatures that have not met

Pretraining-data attribution is a real and active field — it has simply developed alongside hallucination evaluation rather than with it. Influence-based methods now scale to full pretraining corpora: TrackStar traces incorrect factual predictions back to proponent passages across 160B tokens of C4 and categorizes those passages by whether they entail the fact, contain one entity, or contain neither. Verbatim-match infrastructure exists too: OLMoTrace indexes trillions of training tokens and matches output spans in real time — while explicitly refusing to draw the inference this project needs, stating that retrieved documents "should not be interpreted as having a causal effect on the LM output, or as supporting evidence."

A parallel line establishes the statistical relationship in aggregate. Factual accuracy tracks pretraining frequency; entity frequency in Dolma induces asymmetric recall of logically equivalent facts; n-gram coverage of a question predicts whether the model will hallucinate on it. Theory has run ahead of measurement: Kalai et al. derive an expected hallucination rate from the proportion of facts appearing exactly once in the corpus, but validate it only on synthetic data.

Each side holds a piece. None produces what an evaluation needs: a per-instance provenance label, assigned automatically, across a benchmark.

The distinction to state early, before it is asked

Content-based provenance asks whether the fact was literally present in the corpus. It is answered by search, it is cheap, and it is what this project does.

Influence-based attribution asks which training examples shaped the weights that produced this output. It is answered by gradients, it is expensive, and it is what TrackStar and influence functions do.

These answer different questions. Naming the difference in the first paragraphs of related work is the cleanest way to neutralize the objection before a reviewer raises it.

5 · The precise gap

HALoGEN is the only benchmark to propose a taxonomy defined by relationship to pretraining data — Type A for misrecall despite the correct fact being present, Type B for incorrect facts genuinely in the corpus, Type C for fabrication with no basis at all. But it operationalizes that taxonomy through targeted case studies over three scenarios with samples in the low hundreds, and never connects cause to the kind of hallucination observed. The claim this project can defend is therefore narrow and precise: no evaluation suite exists in which every hallucinated claim carries an automatically derived, corpus-grounded provenance label.

6 · Why the gap has practical cost

The three causes demand incompatible remedies. If the correct fact was present and recall failed, retrieval augmentation or a change of decoding strategy addresses it. If the wrong fact was in the corpus, retrieval augmentation actively cannot help — the datastore carries the same error. If nothing was there at all, the only remedy is teaching the model to recognize the edge of its knowledge and decline.

HalluHard supplies direct evidence that undifferentiated mitigation underperforms: enabling web search collapses reference-level failures while leaving content-grounding failures largely intact. That is the signature of a remedy aimed at one cause being applied to a population containing several. Without knowing the mix, mitigation effort is allocated blind.

What kind of root cause

"Root cause analysis" is too vague to design against. Root cause of what, for whom, so that they can do what? In engineering, a root cause is the thing that, once changed, stops the failure recurring — so the question that carries meaning is where would you have to intervene.

A hallucination admits intervention at exactly three points, and Type A/B/C is a statement about which one applies. This is the substantive content of the provenance axis: not an explanation, an address.

LocusInterventionAddressesOwned byCost profile
Corpus Remove or correct the offending documents Type B Whoever builds the dataset One-off, but requires retraining to take effect
Training Increase exposure, upweight rare facts, adjust the objective Type A Type C Whoever trains the model Expensive, slow, affects everything else
Inference Retrieval augmentation, abstention, decoding strategy all three, unequally Whoever deploys Cheap and immediate, but recurring and bounded

We are not looking for the root cause of hallucination in general. We are measuring how a population of hallucinations distributes across the three points where intervention is possible — because those three differ entirely in cost and in who owns them, and no published estimate of the proportions exists.

Read this way, the three causes are not equally interesting to the same person. A corpus curator only cares what fraction is Type B, because that is the entire return on data cleaning. A deployment engineer only cares whether retrieval will help, which depends on how much is Type A. Today all three teams allocate effort against the same population of errors without anyone knowing the split.

On the objection that this is tool-driven research

A fair challenge: did we pick corpus search because it was available, then invent a question to fit it? The framing above answers it. Ask what fraction of hallucination is fixable in the corpus and open-data models stop being a convenience — they become the only instrument that can answer. The question forces the tool, not the other way around.

The discipline this imposes: we measure where a hallucination is addressable, never whether an intervention would succeed. Verifying that a corpus fix removes the error would require retraining, which is out of scope. The phrase "in principle" must survive every draft.

Research questions

For open-weight language models whose pretraining corpora are public: which kinds of hallucination originate from which kinds of training-data condition?

RQ1How does the distribution of failure modes differ across open models? (existence · attribution · non-abstention)
RQ2Within each failure mode, what proportion is Type A, B, or C — that is, at which locus is each addressable? — the central claim
RQ3As model size grows within one family, which cause disappears first?

Contributions

The scope is deliberately shifted away from benchmark construction. Twenty-nine already exist; a thirtieth adds little. This is an explanatory study that reuses existing benchmark material and spends its effort where both literatures stop.

01

The first failure-mode × cause cross-tabulation

core

Every hallucination carries two labels rather than one. The survey above establishes that no existing evaluation suite produces this table.

02

A released cause-labeled hallucination corpus

core

Roughly 2,000 hallucinated units annotated with Type A/B/C plus the corpus evidence behind each judgment. OLMoTrace supplies comparable search but explicitly leaves the labeling undone; this fills exactly that gap.

03

A reusable attribution pipeline and query protocol

supporting

Query generation → corpus search → decision tree → verdict, packaged to run against any model whose pretraining data is public. Includes the protocol for turning an open-ended generated claim into searchable queries, handling paraphrase, and reporting bounds — a methodological problem nobody has yet written down in an orderly way.

04

The first test of the singleton hypothesis against a real corpus

high risk · high reward

Kalai et al. derive expected hallucination rate from the corpus singleton rate — the fraction of facts appearing exactly once in training data. The theory has been validated only on synthetic data, because nobody has held an open corpus and labeled hallucinations at the same time. This project holds both. Bucketing facts by corpus frequency (0 · 1 · 2–10 · 11–100 · 100+) and plotting hallucination rate per bucket tests the prediction directly.

This contribution cannot fail. Agreement with the theory is a first empirical confirmation; disagreement is a first empirical refutation. Both are publishable.

05

A verified survey of 24 hallucination benchmarks

supporting · already complete

Each benchmark checked against its arXiv or ACL source along a dimension nobody has compiled before: whether it inspects the pretraining corpus. The table is not merely related work — it is the empirical evidence that the gap exists.

Weighting — state this explicitly in the thesis

01 and 02 carry the project. If they do not land, nothing else rescues it. 03 and 05 are useful to others but are not the spine. 04 is the asymmetric bet: cheap to run, and interesting whichever way the numbers fall.

Deliberately not listed as contributions: RQ3, because the plan already permits cutting it; the intermediate-checkpoint analysis, because it is optional in Phase 5; and the verifier validation study, which is a section rather than a claim. Taxonomy: two axes

Axis 1 · observable, auto-labeled

Failure mode

EXISTENCECites something that does not exist — a package, a paper, an entity. Verified by index lookup.
ATTRIBUTIONThe source is real but does not support the claim made about it. Verified by retrieval plus entailment.
NON-ABSTENTIONAnswers where it should have declined — false premise, fabricated entity. Verified by gazetteer plus refusal classifier.
Axis 2 · provenance, corpus-searched

Training-data cause

TYPE A — misrecallThe correct fact is in the corpus; retrieval from parameters failed. → addressable at training or inference
TYPE B — bad dataThe incorrect fact genuinely appears in the corpus, or appears stripped of its context. → addressable at the corpus
TYPE C — fabricationNeither correct nor incorrect form is present; the model over-generalized. → addressable only by abstention

Knowledge popularity, model scale, and prompt domain are recorded as metadata, not additional categories — available at analysis time without inflating the label space.

Method: how a cause is assigned

For every hallucinated unit we construct two queries — the string the model produced, and the string of the correct answer — and search both against the pretraining corpus with infini-gram, which indexes Dolma, The Pile, RedPajama and C4 behind a free API at roughly twenty milliseconds per query.

Q_hallucinatedQ_correctVerdictReading
00Type CNothing in the corpus — the model invented it
0> 0Type AThe fact was available; recall failed
> 00Type BThe error is present in the training data
> 0> 0A + BBoth present — reported jointly, never forced to one
Schema requirement — decide in Phase 1, not later

The table above reads as binary, but the pipeline must store the raw occurrence count returned by infini-gram, never the thresholded verdict. Thresholds are applied at analysis time.

Contribution 04 depends entirely on this. Frequency bucketing cannot be reconstructed from binary labels, so a schema that discards counts forces a complete re-run of Phase 4 during Phase 5. It is a one-line decision in week three that is expensive to reverse in week fourteen.

Two honesty requirements

This is evidence-based attribution, not causal proof. Establishing causation would require influence functions over the full training set — the TrackStar approach — which is computationally out of reach here and named as future work by HALoGEN itself. The report must say so plainly.

Exact-string search misses paraphrase. A fact present in another surface form counts as absent and inflates Type C. Mitigation: multiple query forms per fact, and Type C reported as an upper bound rather than a point estimate.

A count alone is insufficient. HALoGEN found repeatedly that hallucinated packages do appear in pretraining data — as someone's local module, as a class inside another library, as a deprecated name. After the automated pass, roughly 100 cases are opened by hand and the observed patterns reported.

If Type B comes out very low, someone on the team will read it as the project having failed. Generalizability and use

Can this be applied to commercial models?

Axis 1 fully; axis 2 not at all. The distinction has to be held firmly, because the temptation to blur it grows the moment closed models enter the conversation.

OperationGPT · Claude · GeminiValidity
Measure failure mode works fully Decided from output alone — identical to open models
Assign Type A/B/C impossible No corpus to search
Substitute a proxy corpus technically possible Done in published work, but nobody has measured how wrong it is
An opportunity that costs almost nothing

Zhang et al. use RedPajama as a stand-in for closed models' data; QuCo-RAG uses OLMo 2's corpus as a stand-in for Llama, Qwen and GPT. Neither can say how much error that substitution introduces, because doing so requires holding the true corpus and a proxy at the same time. We hold both.

Run attribution on OLMo twice — once against Dolma, its actual corpus, and once against RedPajama as though we did not know what it was trained on — then measure label agreement. The output is an error bar on proxy-based attribution, which is exactly the number anyone attempting this on a closed model needs. The infrastructure already exists; infini-gram indexes both. It is one extra run, added to Phase 5.

The ceiling, stated honestly

This project fixes nothing. It measures and explains; impact arrives only when someone else acts on the numbers. Say so plainly rather than claiming reduced hallucination, because the follow-up question — reduced by how much? — has no answer. "We produce the number this decision requires, and nobody currently has it" is both stronger and defensible.

And one line that must not be crossed: closed models may be measured on failure mode and reported as such, but they never enter the cross-tabulation. Their Type A/B/C columns would be guesses wearing the costume of measurements.

Definition of done

  • RQ1 answered across all seven model configurations
  • RQ2 answered for at least OLMo 2 7B — the minimum that constitutes a contribution
  • Verifier agreement ≥ 85% on all three tasks, with κ reported
  • Cause-labeled corpus released with corpus evidence attached to each judgment, raw counts included
  • Hallucination rate reported per corpus-frequency bucket, with the singleton bucket called out
